About Air Quality & Weather Indicators

PM2.5

Fine inhalable particles with diameters that are generally 2.5 micrometers and smaller. They penetrate deep into lungs and can cause respiratory and cardiovascular issues.

PM10

Particulate matter 10 micrometers or less in diameter. Can irritate the eyes, nose, and throat and worsen asthma or heart disease.

CO (Carbon Monoxide)

A colorless, odorless gas. High exposure can reduce oxygen delivery to the body’s organs and tissues, leading to health risks especially for those with heart conditions.

NO₂ (Nitrogen Dioxide)

A reddish-brown gas. Long-term exposure can decrease lung function and increase the risk of respiratory conditions.

SO₂ (Sulfur Dioxide)

Sulfur Dioxide is a toxic gas with a pungent smell. It is mainly released by burning fossil fuels and during industrial processes such as metal smelting. Short-term exposure can lead to respiratory issues and aggravate conditions like asthma. SO₂ also contributes to acid rain.

O₃ (Ozone)

Good in the upper atmosphere but harmful at ground level. It’s a major component of smog and can cause respiratory distress and lung diseases.

Weather Parameters

Temperature, humidity, wind speed, dew point, solar radiation, UV index all influence pollutant dispersion. Calm, cool, and humid conditions can trap pollutants, worsening AQI.
